## Intro

Saw a post on LinkedIn talking about what would be the ideal city in Portugal to establish an engineering hub. While I think it's generally good that this interest exists, I couldn't help myself from annoyingly interject to say that it shouldn't matter, as any serious tech company should be remote first.

As a reply, I got the usual "remote first works well for some", which at this point really annoys me to no end. For this reason, thought about writing this blog post, to try and put in a few more words why I think this is, generally, just a bad excuse.

Remember that I'm focusing on the software development context, though much may also apply in other contexts. Also, I'll be generalizing a lot, which doesn't mean there aren't exceptions.

## Rant (with a hint of nuance)

Now let me get started with ranting, though, as usual, I'll try to include some nuance along the way.

Regarding the specifics of the LinkedIn post I replied to, it's not that I'm against companies having engineering hubs, or any offices for that matter. I think that while these offices aren't generally a real necessity, it's nice that they exist, so there's a place for folks to gather from time to time, or even for the few folks that actually prefer not to work remotely.

My main gripe with the aforementioned post, is the assumption that having everyone gathered in an office, is the right default. It simply isn't.

For starters, the sentence "remote first works well for some", implies the majority only works well when gathered in an office, and only a special few are able to work well remotely. It's the other way around, and I'm tired of people pretending it's not.

Whatever reason there might be for remote work not to be the default for software development, I guarantee you, it has nothing to do with engineering teams being less effective remotely vs onsite, and we've seen that proven again and again.

The very principles that make effective software engineering teams are the same, regardless of where people are located. The only thing I can concede on this, is that an already ineffective team may struggle more, as working remotely can exacerbate existing issues. On the other hand, an effective team can be as effective, if not more, when working remotely.

This whole discussion is so close to irrelevant in the context of software engineering effectiveness, that the [DORA reports](https://dora.dev/research/publications/#prior-state-of-devops-reports) either don't mention it at all, or just mention it very briefly (particularly in reports during the pandemic).

For example, in the 2022 DORA report we find:

> Given the shift to flexible work arrangements that many organizations have adopted since the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic, we investigated whether giving employees the freedom to choose between remote, in-person, or hybrid options was associated with higher organizational performance.
>
> Findings showed that organizations with higher levels of employee flexibility have higher organizational performance compared to organizations with more rigid work arrangements. These findings provide evidence that giving employees the freedom to modify their work arrangements as needed has tangible and direct benefits for an organization

Other than this, and maybe a couple of other very brief mentions of "flexible work arrangements", there's nothing in the report that even tries to correlate team effectiveness with people location. It just isn't a thing.

There are valid reasons to prefer being onsite, be it everyday or on occasion, software engineering teams effectiveness is not one of them.

## Outro

That does it for this little rant.

- Do I think all tech companies should be remote first? No, it's normal to have different preferences.
- Do I think it should be the default, rather than an exception? Absolutely!
- Does my opinion matter to anyone? Clearly not, otherwise the status quo would have changed by now. That won't prevent me from continuing to share it though.

Sorry (not sorry) for the rant. I just get really annoyed with these baseless excuses for forcing everyone into one place, to do a job that can be done from anywhere 🤷‍♂️.
